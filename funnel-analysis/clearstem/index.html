🔑 What to Steal

  1. 1Quiz as TOFU lead capture for skincare. The /pages/quiz destination paired with direct product ads gives CLEARSTEM both cold (segment first, sell later) and warm (skip-to-product) entry points. Skincare buyers vary wildly in skin type / concern — quiz mechanic captures the segmentation data and qualifies the lead before any sell.
  2. 2Creator-IG deep link as social-proof verification. instagram.com/_u/danielle.the.acne.guru — clicking the ad sends the user to the creator's Instagram (not CLEARSTEM's product page). The creator's content does the trust-building work. Same pattern Bettervits used with mother_pukka. Worth borrowing for any category where creator authority matters.
  3. 3Per-SKU dedicated product pages, hero SKU gets the volume. 10+ SKUs covered, but brightmeup takes the largest single share of captured product-ad volume. Coverage spread + spend concentration on the proven winner.
  4. 4International distribution via retailer partners rather than country domains. Bemarket Colombia, Maestro Compras.online, Modeluxe Shop run their own ads selling CLEARSTEM. CLEARSTEM saves the operational complexity of running a global ad account; the retailers absorb local-market knowledge and currency/compliance handling. Different from the Pawfy / Bettervits UK-vs-US ccTLD model.
  5. 5Spanish-language creator co-brand (Dr. Catalina Martínez López). A doctor-named page running Spanish-language CLEARSTEM ads — likely extends US Spanish-speaking reach plus LatAm. The doctor-prefix + native-language creator combines two authority signals.
  6. 6Don't trust simple format filters. The Meta image-vs-video filter said this brand was 60% video / 0% image / 40% unknown. The 40% is real ads — they're in carousel and collection formats Meta filters don't surface. Always check the arithmetic before claiming a creative-format percentage.
