The Strategy

🎯 The Offer Stack

  • 💊Two product lines under one operator: Emma Relief (digestive supplement — the volume leader, 105 active US ads) and Elimipure (also digestion-adjacent based on the destination domain name, 6 active US ads). Heavy concentration on Emma Relief — Elimipure looks like an early-stage or sustaining campaign.
  • 🤰Likely target buyer: Adults with chronic digestive issues — bloating, irregularity, gut health concerns. The fact that the external advertorial domains are "restart your gut" and "restart digestion" suggests a "reset / restart" narrative as the offer hook.
  • 🎨Creative format mix — both video and static across the captured set; no percentage claim because the simple format filter misses carousel/collection formats (today's calibrated lesson — see methodology).
  • 🇪🇸Spanish-language variant: 2 Emma Relief ads route to emmarelief.com/pages/vslfb-es — likely a Spanish-translated landing page (the -es suffix observed but not interpreted further). Suggests US Hispanic-market segmentation.

🔀 The Funnel Architecture — separate external domains per product

This is the headline strategic finding. The operator runs ads to four distinct domains, not two:

  • 🟦emmarelief.com — brand-owned product domain. 104 Emma ads point here (102 to /, 2 to a Spanish-language variant page).
  • 📰restartyourgut.com — external advertorial-style domain (not Enclave-branded). 1 Emma Relief ad routes here.
  • 🟪elimipure.com — Elimipure brand domain. Zero ads in capture point here.
  • 📰restartdigestion.com — external advertorial-style domain (not Elimipure-branded). All 6 Elimipure ads route here.

Strategic observation: Elimipure doesn't route any ad spend to its own brand domain — the entire active campaign runs through an external editorial-style domain. This is the same playbook as Clean Nutraceuticals' Healthnewsline.com page-name trick, but escalated: instead of an editorial-styled FB page name, it's an entirely separate editorial-styled landing-page domain. Reader sees what looks like a content site, not a product site.

🔑 What to Steal

  1. 1External advertorial domains as the actual landing page. Don't route ad spend to your brand-domain product page — route it to a separately-branded editorial domain ("restart your gut," "restart digestion") that reads as content. The user converts on what they perceive as an article, not a product page. Elimipure does this for 100% of spend; Emma Relief uses it as a parallel test.
  2. 2Per-product external domains. Each product line gets its own external advertorial domain, themed to the product's category but not to the product brand. The user can't tell from the URL that they're being sold the same operator's product as the last ad they saw.
  3. 3One operator, two product lines, parallel ad accounts. Emma + Elimipure run as independent ad accounts under separate brand identities. Diversifies operational risk (one account ban doesn't kill the whole operation) while letting one P&L benefit.
  4. 4Hispanic-market landing variant. emmarelief.com/pages/vslfb-es serves Spanish-speaking traffic. Cheap operational add — one translated landing page captures a sizable US Hispanic segment.
  5. 5Concentration on the winner. Emma Relief (105 ads) versus Elimipure (6 ads). Once a product proves out, the spend concentrates massively. Don't keep secondary SKUs at full-volume just because they exist.
  6. 6Holding-company structure obscures the operator. Searching "Enclave BioActives" on FB Ad Library returns nothing — because the consumer-facing brands (Emma, Elimipure) run under their own page names. If you're researching a holding-company brand, search by product, not parent.
