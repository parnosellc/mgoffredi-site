The Strategy

🎯 The Offer & Positioning

  • 💊Multi-SKU supplement operation: products visible in the capture include Vascu-Glow, Gluco-Gone Drops, Shelf Builder Women's Creatine, Para Defend. Each product gets its own landing-page route on cleannutra.com/products/<sku>-bogo.
  • 🇺🇸US-focused: single cleannutra.com domain plus campaign-tracked landing subdomain next.cleannutra.com.
  • 🎨Creative format is mixed — what's visible in the capture is heavily static / carousel: multi-panel benefit grids ("Burns Stored Fat / Reduces Cravings / Balances Blood Sugar / Boosts Metabolism"), Week 1 → Week 2 → Week 3 progression carousels, product hero shots with Shop-Now overlays. Some video creative exists too. Meta's narrow media_type filter undercounts carousel and collection ads — don't trust filter percentages for absolute format claims.
  • 🎁BOGO as the standardized close: every direct-product URL contains -bogo. Buy-one-get-one is the persistent offer architecture, not a seasonal promotion. Standardized close means predictable AOV math.

🔀 The Funnel Architecture (described by ad-creative type, not by URL guess)

  • 🎨Funnel A — Product carousel + benefit-grid creative (20 of 28 captured ads). Ads point to next.cleannutra.com/<uuid> (7+ distinct UUIDs in the capture) — UUID-pathed landing pages whose actual content type isn't verified live in this snapshot. The creatives are mostly multi-panel product showcases, Week-by-week progression carousels, and Shop-Now overlays. MOFU product consideration regardless of where the URL goes.
  • 🛍Funnel B — Direct product BOGO (8 ads): ads to cleannutra.com/products/<sku>-bogo. Same creative archetype as Funnel A; the difference is the URL goes straight to the Shopify-style BOGO product page rather than a campaign-tracked variant.
  • 🥷Healthnewsline.com shadow page (4 ads): same offers pushed under a fake-news page name. The page name itself does the disguise work — the ad reads as a news article to the user.

v1 named Funnel A "Advertorial layer" based on the subdomain pattern matching Pawfy's advertorial URLs. That was URL-slug interpretation, which the SOP forbids. The Pawfy match was coincidental; the actual Clean Nutraceuticals creatives are product ads, not advertorial pre-sell.

🔑 What to Steal

  1. 1UUID-pathed campaign trackers as a default landing-page pattern. next.cleannutra.com/<uuid> per campaign means clean attribution and easy variant testing. Whatever the page renders, the URL pattern itself is a tidy operational habit worth borrowing.
  2. 2BOGO as the standardized close, not a promotion. Every product page is /products/<sku>-bogo. The BOGO is baked into the funnel architecture, not a seasonal sale. Eliminates discount-fatigue and standardizes the offer math.
  3. 3The editorial-mimicry shadow page. Healthnewsline.com as a FB page name makes the ad read as a news article. Aggressive — works on cold traffic wary of brand ads. Use with care: Meta and the FTC both penalize this when it crosses into deceptive territory. A distinct archetype alongside doctor-avatar and house-mom-avatar pages from other brands.
  4. 4Week-by-week carousel as the timeline proof. "Week 1 / Week 2 / Week 3" panel grids let the audience pre-imagine the progression without making explicit before/after claims. Cleaner than a transformation reel; works in regulated categories.
  5. 5Multi-SKU coverage with one landing-page template. Vascu-Glow, Gluco-Gone, Shelf Builder, Para Defend — each gets its own URL but the structure is consistent. Operational leverage: build the template once, regenerate per SKU.
  6. 6Reverse-search exposes the editorial layer. The main page lists ~340 active ads. The keyword search by cleannutra.com is what surfaces the Healthnewsline.com shadow. Without the reverse-search, you miss the most strategically interesting page.
