Ad Naming for Creative

This is the naming system we're moving toward for ad creative. Nothing is using it yet — we're documenting it now so we can roll it out cleanly. Every part of the name tells you something: what product it sells, who built it, what's inside it, what size it is.

What are the pieces?

In human terms

An ad is built from segments — chunks of video that each have a job. A hook to grab attention. A body to deliver the message. A CTA to push action. Sometimes B-roll, testimonials, or extra decorations on top. Each segment gets a code so a name like SL2743 immediately tells anyone "that's hook segment 2743 — same one we used in those four other ads."

Technical — segment codes

CodeNameWhat it is
SLSegment LeadThe hook.
SMSegment MainThe meat-n-potatoes. Often a talking head, sometimes two cut together, sometimes more complex — but always the main building block.
SCSegment CTAThe call to action.
XAExtra AttributeDecorations, weird little things, anything that doesn't fit another category.
XSExtra SnippetB-roll, entertaining clips.
XUUGCUGC, testimonials, workout clips. Treated as one type for simplicity.

Each segment ID is 4 digits.

What information is in the ad name?

  • What we're selling
  • Which creative director requested / supervised the ad
  • Who made the final ad
  • The pieces (segments) it's built from

What are we selling?

In human terms

Every ad sells a product. The product is whatever the buyer pays for at checkout — not the funnel, not the ad copy, not the lander. We sell one product right now: the Toolkit. So every ad name starts with tk.

Technical

Two-letter lowercase product code. Only one in use today: tk. A new code is added only when a genuinely different product appears at checkout.

Who requested it? (Creative director)

In human terms

The creative director who briefed the ad or supervised the edit. Not the person who cut it — that's the editor, next section. Right now there are two: Erick Argueta (eag) and SSR (ssr).

Technical

3-letter lowercase code, preceded by the fixed prefix cd. So Erick becomes cdeag, SSR becomes cdssr. Sits right after the product code, before the editor block.

Who made the ad?

In human terms

The person who exported the final cut. Not the writer, not the videographer — whoever tied the pieces together.

Technical

3-letter code, uppercase. Each editor picks their own. Example: Michael = MGO.

What's NOT in the ad name

The funnel, the landing page, the campaign — none of this goes in the filename. It's tracked separately:

  • In Airtable, on the creative record's "Eligible Landing Pages" field.
  • In Frame.io, on the custom field per asset.

The reason: the same ad can run to multiple landers. Keeping the lander out of the name means one file, many possible destinations. Putting it in the name would force us to duplicate files every time we wanted to test a new placement.

Series and version letters (the 174g part)

In human terms

You make ads in batches — variations off the same hook, sweeps of CTA tests. We call each batch a "series" and number them. Within a series, each individual ad gets a letter (a, b, c…).

Technical

3-digit series number + lowercase letter. Series numbering is per-editor — MGO174 and JOC174 are different series, owned by different people.

Putting it all together

Simple ad

tk_cdeag_MGO174g_SL2743_SM2067_SC1542_XA1180_9x16

Decoded:

  • tk — Toolkit
  • cdeag — Erick Argueta requested/supervised (creative director)
  • MGO174g — Michael, series 174, ad version g
  • SL2743 — hook segment 2743
  • SM2067 — body segment 2067
  • SC1542 — CTA segment 1542
  • XA1180 — extra decoration 1180
  • 9x16 — final aspect ratio

Multi-segment ad

tk_cdeag_MGO174g_SL2743_SL2772_SM2067_SM2057_SM2280_SC1542_XA1178_XA1180_XA1258_9x16

Same logic, more pieces stitched together. Two hooks tested, three main bodies layered, one CTA, three decorations.
