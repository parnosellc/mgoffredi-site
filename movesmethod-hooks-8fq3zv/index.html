The mechanic — a pre-intro scene

Add a short new scene to the front of each ad. The ad then plays as normal.

PRE-INTRO · 2–5 sec
"…until I saw this."
→
THE AD
plays as normal — intro, body, CTA

Why it works: the hook ends by pointing at the ad. "I didn't know X until I saw this" — and then "this" plays. The pre-intro stops the scroll and introduces the ad.

The layman's wrong-fix list (a frame, not a stat)

What grandma actually tries: cut the sugar · switch to diet soda · swap to oatmeal & wheat bread · eat fruit instead · walk after dinner · lose 20 pounds · cinnamon & apple cider vinegar · take the pill and consider it handled.

Not one item on that list involves muscle. Her whole model is input-side — blood sugar is a thing you eat. Nobody tells her the muscles are the organ that clears the glucose, and hers have been shrinking for twenty years. That's the blind spot, and it's real, not manufactured.

The recipe

  • Pull the best segments from the winners above — hooks, demos, mechanism explainers, testimonials, b-roll.
  • Stack a Task 1 pre-intro hook on the front. Any of the "…until I saw this" lines.
  • Speed some clips up. The fc_mgo winners literally have speed1.25 in their filenames — already a proven setting here.
  • Use the FCVSL "watch the free training" CTA — that's the close working on the biggest spenders.
  • DOUBLE CTA — ask for the click twice. Once mid-roll, once at the end. Don't save the ask for the end.
  • The IG VIRAL manifesto makes a great tail-CTA — bolt it on as the second close.

Production

Notes for editors

  • Pre-intro length: 2–5 seconds. Keep it tight — run long and you bury the ad's own hook.
  • Match the cut. End on "…until I saw this" and hard-cut into the ad's first frame. The seam should feel like a reveal.
  • Pre-intro visual: talking head, or a visceral frame — X-ray pain-glow, a failed floor-rise, sugar poured on a joint, an adult child lifting a parent out of a chair. Weird beats pretty.
  • One variable per test. Same base ad, different pre-intro. Clean read on the hook alone.
  • Compliance: show the fear, imply the medical claim. Debunk the walk, the oatmeal, the fruit, the diet soda — never tell anyone to stop taking medication.
