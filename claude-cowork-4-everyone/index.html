🎯 What is Claude Cowork?

You're about to set up your own AI assistant. By the end of the first half of this course, you'll have:

  • Claude installed on your computer with access to a folder you control
  • An assistant that remembers you between conversations
  • A daily 9am check-in that helps you build the habit of using it

The three tabs. When you open the Claude desktop app, you'll see three tabs at the top:

  • Chat — like ChatGPT. You ask, it answers. Conversation ends, memory ends.
  • Cowork — what we're setting up today. Has access to a folder on your computer. Remembers you. Does multi-step tasks.
  • Code — for software developers. Ignore for now.

🔌 Install Claude Cowork

Four steps. Do them in order — the order matters.

1Subscribe to Claude first

Go to claude.ai/upgrade and subscribe to Claude Pro before downloading the desktop app. There's a bug where Cowork doesn't show up if you download first and subscribe later — it can take a while to sort itself out. Save yourself the headache: subscribe first.

Checkpoint: You see "Pro" (or "Max") next to your name on claude.ai.

2Update your operating system

Mac: Apple menu → System Settings → General → Software Update.
Windows: Start → Settings → Windows Update.
Run all available updates. Restart if it asks. Cowork needs a recent OS.

Checkpoint: Your OS reports it's up to date.

3Download Claude Desktop

Go to claude.ai/download and install like any normal app — drag to Applications on Mac, or run the installer on Windows. Sign in with the account you just subscribed.

Checkpoint: Claude Desktop is open and you're signed in.

4Confirm all three tabs show up

At the top of the Claude Desktop window, you should see three tabs side by side: Chat, Cowork, and Code. That's the success signal — it should look exactly like this:

![Three-tab toolbar showing Chat, selected Cowork, and Code](cowork-tabs-success.png)

If you only see one or two tabs, your Pro subscription hasn't propagated yet. Wait 5-10 minutes, sign out and back in, restart the app. If it's still missing after 20 minutes, double-check your subscription went through on claude.ai.

Checkpoint: You see all three tabs. Click "Cowork." That's where we're going next.

📁 Assign Your Folder

This is the most important step in the course. The thing that makes Cowork actually Cowork instead of just another chatbot.

Why a folder? Chat assistants forget you the moment you close the window. Cowork remembers — but only because it can read and write files in a folder on your computer that you give it access to. Your assistant's memory lives on your hard drive, in plain English files you can open in Word or read on your phone.

How to assign one:

  1. In the Cowork tab, look for Settings (gear icon, or under a menu in the corner).
  2. Find the option called Workspace folder (sometimes "Working folder" or just "Folder").
  3. Click it. You'll get a normal Mac or Windows folder-picker.
  4. Recommended: Don't pick an existing folder full of work documents. Make a fresh folder on your Desktop called My Claude. Easy to find, doesn't mix with anything else.

Confirm it worked. Drop a Word doc or text file into the folder you just picked. Then go to Cowork and type:

"Read the file in my folder and tell me what it says."

If Claude reads it back, you're connected. If not, double-check the folder you picked is actually the one Cowork is using.

🎤 Bootstrap Your Cowork

Now you paste one prompt into your Cowork and it sets itself up. Names itself, interviews you, saves your context, and schedules a daily check-in.

What's about to happen
  1. Your Cowork will warmly say hi and explain the setup (~15 min).
  2. It'll ask what to call it — pick a name.
  3. It'll ask a few quick questions about how you want it to talk to you.
  4. It'll interview you about your work — one question at a time, ~10 minutes.
  5. It'll save what you told it to a file in your folder so it remembers you forever.
  6. It'll create a starter to-do list with a few real things to try.
  7. It'll schedule a daily 9am check-in to help you finish the to-do list one task at a time.

Every weekday at 9am after that, your Cowork pings you with one open task. You answer "done," "not yet," or "skip." That's the rhythm.

🎧 Listen: What the Paste Does

Copy the prompt below and paste it into your Cowork. Send it. Then follow your assistant's lead.

Hi! I'm setting you up as my work assistant for the first time. I'm not particularly technical. I use Word and Excel for most things. Please go slowly, ask me one thing at a time, and use plain language. No jargon.

I'd like you to set yourself up by walking me through four things today. You'll know we're done when you confirm all of them.

PART A — Name and personality

First: ask me what I want to call you. That's your name from now on.

Then ask me three short questions, one at a time:
  1. Should you talk to me formally or casually?
  2. What language should we work in? (English by default — or tell me another language if you'd prefer that)
  3. Should you be friendly and conversational, or efficient and brief?

Write down my answers.

PART B — Interview me, one question at a time

  1. What's your name?
  2. What do you do for work? Tell me in plain words.
  3. What does a typical week look like for you?
  4. What tools do you use day-to-day? (Word, Excel, Outlook, Gmail, Google Drive, anything else)
  5. Are there a few different projects or clients you work with, or mostly one thing?
  6. What kind of tasks take up most of your time?
  7. What's the most annoying or repetitive thing in your work — the thing you'd hand off first if you could?
  8. Anything else I should know about you to be useful?

PART C — Set up my files

  - Save my answers from Part A and Part B to a file called `about-me.md` at the top of my workspace folder. Use clear headings. From now on, every time we work together, read that file first so you remember me.
  - Create a file called `todo.md` at the top of my workspace folder with exactly these starter tasks:

      # My To-Do List

      Each item:
      - [ ] **Task title** — what it is
        - Status: open
        - Last asked: never
        - Notes:

      ---

      ## Starter tasks (we'll work through these together)

      - [ ] **Show me a real piece of your work** — Drop a document, email, screenshot, or note into your workspace folder and tell me what you need help with. This is your first real win.
        - Status: open
        - Last asked: never
        - Notes:

      - [ ] **Connect your email** — In Cowork settings → Connectors, sign in with Outlook or Gmail. This lets me read your inbox to help with replies.
        - Status: open
        - Last asked: never
        - Notes:

      - [ ] **Tell me about one specific recurring task** — Pick one thing you do every week and walk me through it. I'll see if I can make it faster.
        - Status: open
        - Last asked: never
        - Notes:

      - [ ] **Add a second project folder, if you have multiple projects/clients** — Tell me you want to add another, and I'll create the folder structure with you.
        - Status: open
        - Last asked: never
        - Notes:

PART D — Schedule the daily check-in

Create a scheduled task that runs every weekday at 9:00am in my local timezone with this prompt:

---SCHEDULED PROMPT START---

Good morning. Read `about-me.md` and `todo.md` first.

Find the OLDEST task with status "open". Just one. Ignore the rest.

Greet me warmly in one short sentence using my name. Then ask me about that one task in plain language. Something like:

"Morning! Today's open one is still: [task title]. Where are you with it — done, not yet, or want to skip it?"

Wait for my reply. Then:

- If I say "done" or anything that confirms completion → mark it [x], change status to "done", note today's date, write one warm one-liner, and tell me what's next on the list (preview only — don't ask about it yet).

- If I say "not yet" or describe a blocker → keep it open, update "Last asked" to today, save any notes to "Notes:". Ask if I want to do it RIGHT NOW with your help. If yes, walk me through it. If no, say "ok, tomorrow then" and end.

- If I say "skip" or "not relevant" → change status to "skipped", note the date, ask briefly why (one short question), save my answer to Notes. Don't argue.

- If my reply is vague, ask ONE clarifying question. Don't pile on.

Tone: warm, brief, peer-level. Like a calm friend who knows I'm capable.

End every check-in by saving the updated `todo.md`. That's how tomorrow's check-in knows where we are.

---SCHEDULED PROMPT END---

After all four parts are done, confirm out loud:
  1. The name I picked for you
  2. The three personality settings
  3. That `about-me.md` and `todo.md` both exist in my folder
  4. That the daily 9am check-in is scheduled

Then say: "We're set up. Your first real task is in your to-do list — drop something you're working on into the folder and tell me what you need. I'll be here. And I'll see you tomorrow morning at 9 for our first check-in."

🧠 How It Remembers

The strangest thing about your new assistant: it doesn't actually remember anything between conversations. Each new chat starts completely fresh.

The trick that solves it: your workspace folder is its memory. Anything you want it to remember, you save as a file. Next time you open a conversation, it reads your files first and catches up.

You've already seen this. The about-me.md file you just created is its onboarding manual. It reads that first every session, so it remembers who you are. The todo.md file holds your to-do list across days.

The rule: anything important, ask it to save. A draft you like? Save it. A useful note about a project? Save it. The folder is the memory; the conversation is just where you talk.

Phrases to use
  • "Always read my about-me.md before we start working."
  • "Save this as a file so we have it next time."
  • "If you forget something we discussed, look in the folder first."

There's a smaller version of this within a single chat: if a conversation gets very long, your assistant starts to forget things from earlier in the same chat. When that happens, start a new chat and let it re-read your folder. Fresh start, same notes.

🗣️ Talking to Your Assistant

This is the highest-leverage chapter. Master these three habits and your assistant goes from "okay" to genuinely useful.

Most people open the chat, type something like "write me an email about X," hit send, get a mediocre response, and blame the AI. The fix isn't more AI knowledge — it's three small habits.

Habit 1 — Give context before you ask. Don't lead with the request. Lead with the situation. Bad: "Write a pitch." Good: "I work at a credit ratings company. We released a survey on small business credit trends. I need to pitch this to fintech journalists. The tone should feel newsworthy, not promotional. Now draft me a pitch." Same task, dramatically better output.

Habit 2 — Make it ask you questions first. Try: "Before you draft anything, ask me three questions to make sure you understand." It'll ask. Answering forces both of you to anchor on what actually matters. The output after the interview is twice as good as the cold output.

Habit 3 — Push back when it's off. If a draft is too corporate, tell it. "Shorter. Less formal. Punchier opening. Drop the second paragraph." It's not insulted. It just redoes it. The more you correct, the more it learns your voice.

A few other moves that consistently work:

  • Ask for two options instead of one — comparison makes choosing easier than judging a single draft.
  • Tell it to act as a specific role: "Act as an editor and tighten this paragraph."
  • Tell it what good looks like: "A good pitch is under 120 words, has a number in the subject line, and ends with one clear ask." Now it has something to hit.
  • When it nails something — tell it: "That's the right tone. Remember this voice for future work."
Phrases to memorize
  • "Before you start, ask me three questions to make sure you understand."
  • "That's too corporate. Redo it shorter."
  • "Show me two options, not one."
  • "Act as a [specific role] and rewrite this."

If you take one thing from this whole course, take this: a sharp prompt beats a smart AI. The job is to set it up well.

🔗 Connectors

A connector is a wire between your assistant and an app you already use. Outlook, Gmail, Google Drive, Slack, your calendar. Once it's connected, your assistant can read from that app on your behalf — and sometimes write to it.

How to add one: in Cowork settings → Connectors. You'll see what's available. Click sign-in for each app you want to plug in. Microsoft for Outlook. Google for Gmail, Drive, Calendar.

Important default: most connectors start in read-only mode. Your assistant can see your inbox but can't send anything on its own. Drafts get saved to your folder; you send manually. That's intentional. Once you're comfortable, you can expand permissions.

Here's a real example of what the email connector lets you do. You sent five emails this week. Three days later you want to know who replied. You ask:

"Check my inbox for replies in the last week. Summarize what each one says in one line."

Your assistant runs the search, lists the replies, asks which to respond to first. You pick one. It drafts a reply in your folder. You polish and send.

Phrases to use (after Outlook/Gmail is connected)
  • "Search my inbox for messages from [person] in the last week."
  • "Draft a reply to this — save it in my folder, don't send."
  • "What emails haven't I responded to yet?"

Don't add every connector on day one. Each one adds capability but also adds surface area for things to go sideways. One at a time, when you have a real reason.

✅ Trust, but Verify

Your assistant is right most of the time. Not always. Sometimes it'll be confidently wrong about a fact — a statistic, a date, a person's role, a contact detail. It's not common but it happens. The technical word for it is "hallucination." The practical word is "confident-but-wrong."

This isn't something to be afraid of. It's something to build a five-second habit around.

The principle. Facts that came FROM you (your files, your notes, what you typed in this chat) — those are fine. Facts that came FROM your assistant's own knowledge (a stat it remembers, a fact about a person, a date it cites) — those are drafts until you've eyeballed them.

The trick is making it tell you which is which.

Phrases to teach your assistant
  • "For every fact in your answer, tell me where it came from — my files, my notes, or your general knowledge."
  • "Flag anything you're not sure about. Don't smooth it over."
  • "If you don't know something, say I don't know instead of guessing."
  • "Tell me what you're least sure about in this draft."

The "I don't know" one is the most important. A good assistant says it. A weak one fakes it. You can train yours toward the former just by asking for it.

For any fact you'll act on — a contact detail, a quote, a citation, a date — verify it. Same habit you already use when checking junior work before it goes out.

🚫 What It Can't Do

A short, honest list of limits so you can plan around them.

  • Can't make phone calls. Voice is still you.
  • Can't predict the future. It can suggest, draft, analyze — but it can't tell you whether a pitch will land, a strategy will work, or a person will respond.
  • Can't replace your judgment. It drafts; you decide. Same way a senior overrides a junior's work.
  • Doesn't know recent events. Its knowledge has a cutoff date. For anything time-sensitive — recent news, a regulatory change — check, or paste the latest source in.
  • Can be wrong about facts. See the previous chapter. Verify what matters.

The frame to leave with: a great assistant makes you faster. The best assistants also know when to step back. Yours is trying to be the second kind. If it ever tries to make a call that's really yours to make — push back. Make it work on the inputs, not the verdicts.

Phrases to teach it
  • "If you can't actually do this task, tell me — don't fake it."
  • "For anything recent, check before you answer."
  • "This is my call to make. Just give me the inputs."

☕ Building the Habit

Most people who try AI tools quit in week two. Not because the tools are bad — because they never built the habit. The tools sit there. Don't be that person.

Here's the daily rhythm that works.

Morning (5 minutes):

  • Open Cowork.
  • Name your project, if you have more than one: "I'm working on [project] today."
  • Ask: "Walk me through yesterday's tracker. What moved? What's left?"
  • Set today's top three priorities.

End of day (2 minutes):

  • Log what happened. Who replied. What got finished. What's owed tomorrow.

That's the whole ritual. Five minutes morning, two minutes evening. The daily 9am check-in you set up earlier will start the morning ritual for you — don't skip it. Two weeks of this and your assistant stops being a thing you "use" and becomes a colleague you work with.

You'll know it's working when two things happen. First, you stop having that "wait, what was I doing again?" moment in the middle of the day — the tracker holds it for you. Second, you stop thinking about it as "AI" at all. You just ask your assistant for the thing and keep moving.

Welcome to the new way of working.
