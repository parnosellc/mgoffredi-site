🗑️ What we removed (and why)

These were the things most likely to get the ad — or the whole account — shut down.

  • ⏳ The countdown timer + "discount expiring soon."
    It reset on every visit, so it wasn't a real deadline. Google treats that as fake pressure.
  • 🩺 "Avoided surgery entirely" + the cured-conditions list (torn ACLs, herniated discs, sciatica…).
    These are medical promises. A fitness product can't claim to treat or cure medical conditions. This was the riskiest thing on the page.
  • ✨ Cure-style promises — "eliminate pain forever," "feel 10 years younger."
    No product can guarantee an outcome like that.
  • ⭐ The hand-built Trustpilot lookalike (fake green stars, recreated logo, hardcoded review cards).
    It copied Trustpilot's branding, and the typed-in numbers can't update so they drift out of sync with the real profile — a trademark + misrepresentation problem. See "How to show Trustpilot reviews" below for what to use instead.
  • 🔢 Conflicting headcounts (107,000 in one spot, 200,000 in another).
    We kept one number everywhere. Numbers that don't match make the whole page look made up.
  • 🚨 Emoji + ALL-CAPS hype ("BREAKING NEWS" etc.).
    Google's quality rules specifically frown on that gimmicky style.

✏️ What we reworded (and why)

Same hook and value — just stated in a way Google allows.

  • "The Hidden Joint Problem" → "The joint problem most routines miss."
    "Hidden" reads like clickbait / "secret trick" language that Google's filters flag, even for a legit product.
  • "…have Fixed it in 10 minutes a day" → "…have used to move better."
    Turned a guaranteed result into a realistic, qualified one.
  • "Prevent the pain from coming back" / "without even warming up" → "tends to hold up better over time" / "move more freely."
    Same idea, stated as a likely benefit, not a promise.
  • Testimonials — kept them, trimmed the strongest claims, added a small "results vary" line.
    Testimonials that imply "you'll get this too" need that caveat.

📐 Why the new page is shorter

The clean page is intentionally leaner. A few whole sections didn't make the cut — for two different reasons.

Cut for compliance — stays gone

  • The "Spring Sale – Save $180" bar and countdown timer — fake urgency.
  • The "BREAKING NEWS" section — the fake-news style and "quick band-aid / you're not just getting old" claims are exactly what Google flags.

Cut to stay clean & focused — not banned

  • The long problem/agitation section (pain-point bullets, red-X / green-check images).
  • The "This is for you if you're…" block (40s / fitness enthusiast / athlete).
  • The long founder story ("$100k spent, 40,000 clients, 1M reached") — shortened to a brief, honest coach intro.
  • Repeated pitch and duplicated "What's included" blocks — the old page said both twice; we said them once.

To be clear: the persuasion sections aren't banned. Standard agitation, a "who it's for" block, and a founder story are all allowed — the originals were just packed with the risky claims and inflated numbers. We chose a short, video-and-offer-first page for Step 1. The longer version can be rebuilt compliantly later.

⭐ How to show Trustpilot reviews

For whoever builds the page. A real Trustpilot widget is 100% allowed — a hand-built copy is not. Pick the option that matches whatever Trustpilot plan we're on (the design/copy team will know which).

🚫 What was wrong with the old one

The old page redrew Trustpilot by hand — fake green stars, a recreated logo, hardcoded review cards. Two problems: it borrows Trustpilot's trademarked branding without using their actual widget, and the numbers are typed in by hand so they drift out of sync with the live profile. Google reads that as misrepresentation. Never recreate Trustpilot's look manually.

✅ Option 1 — Best (paid Trustpilot plan)

Use the official Trustpilot TrustBox widget. Trustpilot generates the embed code; paste it on the page. It pulls live reviews + score, carries the real logo legitimately, and updates itself so it can never drift. Note: the review-displaying TrustBox widgets need a paid Trustpilot plan (~$259–319/mo).

✅ Option 2 — Free plan

Use Trustpilot's official clickable logo badge + plain text like "Rated 4.8/5 on Trustpilot," linked to our live profile. Use their official badge asset — not self-drawn stars — and make sure the score matches the live profile.

❌ Never

A hand-built replica of Trustpilot's stars, logo, or review cards with typed-in numbers.

Separate housekeeping: Trustpilot has flagged this account for collecting reviews in a way they don't support. That's about how reviews are requested, not how they're shown — worth cleaning up so the profile stays in good standing either way.

🛠️ Also make sure (for the build team)

Easy things to miss that will still get the page flagged.

  • 🖼️ Text inside images and videos counts too.
    Google reads text baked into images and judges the VSL itself. A clean page with claim cards like "PAIN GONE," or a video that says "eliminate pain forever," still gets flagged. Apply all these rules to image overlays and the video script — not just the body copy.
  • 💲 The $247 strike-through must be a real price.
    It has to be a price the product is genuinely sold at. If not, drop it and just show $27.
  • 📝 The disclaimers have to actually ship.
    "Results vary" near the testimonials and "not medical advice — consult a professional" in the footer aren't optional polish. They need to be on the live page.
  • 🔢 One social-proof number across the whole funnel.
    Pick one (e.g. 107,000) and use it on every page, the ads, and the video. 107k / 200k / 141k still show up in different places.
